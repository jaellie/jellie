## 2. What stops the tower clock at 11:47  (🟡 physical mechanism)

- It is an old **weight-driven pendulum clock.** The pendulum hangs in a shaft beside the wheel pit; a **crutch** links it to the escapement anchor.
- Edward falls through the hatch onto the **going-train platform** and strikes the **pendulum rod**, **bending it** and **knocking the crutch off the anchor pin.**
- With the pendulum stopped, the escapement stops within a second; the train stops; **the dial freezes at 11:47.**
- **Physical evidence the player can find (EV-25):** bent pendulum rod; crutch pin sheared; a tuft of **Edward's quills** and a thread of his vest wool caught on the rod; a smear of blood on the platform edge.
- It shows **when the fall happened**, not when Edward died.

## 3. The pocket watch  (🟡 recommendation: **it does not stop**)

Your instruction: remove the artificial second impact; reconsider whether the watch must stop at all.

**Recommendation:** the watch **keeps running.** It still does real work:
1. **A reliable running clock.** The tower clock is frozen, so the player needs a second time base. The Clockkeeper syncs the watch to the tower every morning (Q8), so it is trustworthy.
2. **Physical evidence of the fall.** The crystal is **cracked** and the case **dented** on one side (Edward landed on his left hip). That supports EV-25 and the injury picture; it says nothing about death time.
3. **It anchors the Healer's and Clockkeeper's times** ("00:14 by Edward's watch"), independent of the stopped tower.
4. The "stopped clock vs running watch" contrast is itself the lesson: **no clock tells you when he died.**

Alternatives: **(b)** it stops from cold (a cold-thickened mainspring oil), which is real but too slow and imprecise to be a timestamp; **(c)** it stops at the fall (11:47), duplicating the tower and adding nothing. I recommend **no stop.** **D14's earlier lock ("stops at 11:52") is therefore revoked and open again (R4).**
